It sounds like you have solid goals and a good foundation to work with, despite the setback from COVID-19 and other commitments. Here's a two-month training plan tailored to help you get back into competitive running and achieve your goals:

**Week 1-2: Recovery and Base Building**
- Focus on easy runs at a comfortable pace to rebuild your mileage and stamina.
- Aim for 4-5 days of running per week, gradually increasing mileage from 20-30 miles to around 35-40 miles by the end of Week 2.
- Incorporate cross-training like cycling or swimming to maintain cardiovascular fitness without overloading your body.
- Include flexibility and strength exercises to prevent injuries and improve overall athleticism.

**Week 3-4: Introduce Speed Work**
- Start incorporating speed workouts such as intervals, fartleks, or tempo runs.
- Example workout: 4x800m at 5k pace with 2 minutes recovery jog in between.
- Continue building mileage, aiming for 40-45 miles by the end of Week 4.
- Maintain 4-5 days of running per week with 1-2 rest or cross-training days.

**Week 5-6: Increase Intensity**
- Increase the intensity and volume of speed workouts while maintaining a balance with easy runs.
- Example workout: 6x400m at mile race pace with 90 seconds recovery jog.
- Consider adding hill repeats or longer tempo runs to build strength and endurance.
- Gradually increase mileage to 45-50 miles per week.
- Focus on recovery with proper nutrition, hydration, and sleep.

**Week 7-8: Peak Performance**
- Fine-tune your training by simulating race conditions and pacing in workouts.
- Incorporate race-pace runs and tune-up races (if possible) to gauge your fitness level.
- Example workout: 3-mile tempo run at half-marathon goal pace.
- Taper down mileage and intensity in the final week leading up to your goal race.
- Emphasize rest and recovery to ensure fresh legs on race day.
- Visualize your race goals and develop a race strategy.

**Additional Tips:**
- Listen to your body and adjust your training plan accordingly to prevent overtraining or injury.
- Stay consistent with your training but don't be afraid to take extra rest days if needed, especially during recovery from COVID-19.
- Focus on nutrition to fuel your workouts and optimize recovery.
- Set realistic expectations for your first marathon based on your training progress and previous race performances.
- Consider consulting with a coach or experienced runner for personalized guidance and support throughout your training journey.
